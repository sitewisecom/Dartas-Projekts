# The Classical Period in Western Music (c. 1750–1820)

> Source note: Britannica (HTTP 403) and the Metropolitan Museum essay (HTTP 429) could not be fetched, and Grove Music Online is paywalled. Most findings below come from Wikipedia and a LibreTexts university music-appreciation textbook, cross-checked between pages where possible. Catalogue numbers and dates for the five pieces were each checked against the piece's own article. Wikipedia flags parts of its Classical-period article as uncited, so treat style generalisations as textbook consensus, not primary scholarship.

## 1. Dates, Enlightenment context and style features

### Takeaway
The dates "c. 1750–1820" are a convention, not a hard border. Sources variously give 1730, 1750 or 1775 as the start and 1820 or the late 1820s as the end. The style replaced Baroque polyphony with a clear melody over simple chords, short balanced phrases and clearly marked cadences, matching the Enlightenment taste for order, clarity and "natural" simplicity.

### Cited Findings
- **The dates are disputed.** The Wikipedia lead says "roughly 1750 and 1820", while its infobox says c. 1730–1820. Scholar James F. Daugherty dates a "Viennese Classic period" to about 1775–1825. — [Wikipedia: Classical period (music)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_period_(music))
- A university textbook gives "about 1750–1820" but uses 1730–1820 for its own scope. It places the era between Baroque and Romantic. — [LibreTexts, Music Appreciation II, 13.01](https://human.libretexts.org/Bookshelves/Music/Music_Appreciation/Music_Appreciation_II_(Kuznetsova)/13%3A_The_Classical_Era_Classical_Forms_and_Symphony_J._Haydn/13.01%3A_Overview_of_the_Classical_Era_of_Music)
- **The Baroque is dated c. 1600–1750.** Italian composers had moved to the galant style around 1730, while German composers such as J. S. Bach "largely continued to write in the baroque style up to 1750". The article notes "appreciable disagreement about time boundaries". — [Wikipedia: Baroque music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baroque_music)
- **There was a transition, not a sharp break.** The Baroque-to-Classical transition is put at roughly 1750–1760. The Classical style faded gradually, and Vienna's orchestral pre-eminence saw its "final eclipse" in the late 1820s after the deaths of Beethoven (1827) and Schubert (1828). — [Wikipedia: Classical period (music)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_period_(music))
- **Intellectual context.** Mid-18th-century Europe turned to Neoclassicism, which imitated the ideals of classical antiquity (especially Greece). The sources tie this to Newtonian "natural philosophy", which prized well-founded, orderly structures. (The textbook itself does not use the word "Enlightenment".) — [LibreTexts 13.01](https://human.libretexts.org/Bookshelves/Music/Music_Appreciation/Music_Appreciation_II_(Kuznetsova)/13%3A_The_Classical_Era_Classical_Forms_and_Symphony_J._Haydn/13.01%3A_Overview_of_the_Classical_Era_of_Music); [Wikipedia: Classical period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_period_(music))
- **Precursor styles.** The *style galant* favoured light elegance over Baroque grandeur. *Empfindsamkeit* (the "sensitive style") was led by C. P. E. Bach, whose music was expressive and often abrupt. — [Wikipedia: Classical period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_period_(music))
- **Style features:**
  - Texture shifted from polyphony to homophony: a clear melody "over a subordinate harmony" (chordal accompaniment).
  - Textures were lighter and clearer, with shorter melodies, clear phrases and clearly marked cadences.
  - Dynamics and phrasing were notated more carefully.
  - Contrasts of key, rhythm, dynamics, mood and timbre became greater.
  
  — [LibreTexts 13.01](https://human.libretexts.org/Bookshelves/Music/Music_Appreciation/Music_Appreciation_II_(Kuznetsova)/13%3A_The_Classical_Era_Classical_Forms_and_Symphony_J._Haydn/13.01%3A_Overview_of_the_Classical_Era_of_Music)
- **Phrases, bass and continuo.** Phrases were typically four bars long, with tonic–dominant contrast. Accompaniment was often an **Alberti bass** (broken-chord figure). The basso continuo was "nearly extinct by 1800". — [Wikipedia: Classical period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_period_(music)); [LibreTexts 13.01](https://human.libretexts.org/Bookshelves/Music/Music_Appreciation/Music_Appreciation_II_(Kuznetsova)/13%3A_The_Classical_Era_Classical_Forms_and_Symphony_J._Haydn/13.01%3A_Overview_of_the_Classical_Era_of_Music)

### Inferences
- **On the 1750 start.** It is best presented as "the conventional symbolic start, around the time of J. S. Bach's death". The galant style that leads into Classicism was already under way by the 1730s, so a narrator should say "around 1750".
- **On the 1820 end.** It is likewise a convention. Using 1827 (Beethoven's death) is a defensible alternative, and the late 1820s are the latest commonly cited end point.

### Gaps
- None of the fetched pages gave J. S. Bach's exact death date. It is widely known as 28 July 1750, but this should be confirmed (for example on Britannica) before the narration uses it.
- The Britannica and Met essays could not be accessed, so no direct quotation from them is available.

## 2. New and standardised forms, instruments and the orchestra

### Takeaway
Sonata form became the central structure of the era. The symphony, string quartet and solo concerto became the main instrumental genres. Gluck reformed opera with *Orfeo ed Euridice* (Vienna, 1762). The piano displaced the harpsichord, and the orchestra settled into a standard shape (strings plus an independent wind section), helped by the Mannheim orchestra's example.

### Cited Findings
- **Forms and genres.**
  - Sonata form became the most important form, used mainly for first movements, and also in symphonies, quartets and overtures.
  - The main genres were the sonata, trio, string quartet, quintet, symphony, concerto, serenade and divertimento.
  - The concerto grosso gave way to the solo concerto, with more virtuoso display.
  
  — [Wikipedia: Classical period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_period_(music)); [LibreTexts 13.01](https://human.libretexts.org/Bookshelves/Music/Music_Appreciation/Music_Appreciation_II_(Kuznetsova)/13%3A_The_Classical_Era_Classical_Forms_and_Symphony_J._Haydn/13.01%3A_Overview_of_the_Classical_Era_of_Music)
- **The orchestra.** It grew in size and range. The string section was standardised, the woodwinds became a self-contained section, and the harpsichord continuo dropped out. — [Wikipedia: Classical period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_period_(music))
- **Mannheim school.**
  - Mannheim was the court orchestra of the Elector Palatine. Johann Stamitz was hired in 1741/42 and became its director in 1750.
  - Charles Burney called it "an army of generals".
  - Its trademark effects were the Mannheim crescendo, the "Rocket" (a fast rising figure with a crescendo), the "Sigh" and the Grand Pause.
  - It helped establish the four-movement symphony with a minuet as third movement, and it contributed to sonata form. Its influence reached Haydn.
  - Mozart praised the orchestra in letters of 1777–78, and the finale of his Symphony No. 40 opens with a Rocket.
  - Similar changes happened almost at the same time in Berlin and Vienna.
  
  — [Wikipedia: Mannheim school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mannheim_school)
- **Opera reform (Gluck).**
  - *Orfeo ed Euridice* premiered at the **Burgtheater, Vienna, on 5 October 1762**, before Empress Maria Theresa, with a libretto by Ranieri de' Calzabigi. It was the first of Gluck's reform operas.
  - Its aim was "noble simplicity", replacing opera seria's convoluted plots and over-ornate music. In practice that meant few da capo arias, no secco recitative, fewer subplots, a prominent chorus and expressive dance.
  - Gluck was influenced by Algarotti's *Essay on the Opera* (1755).
  - A revised French version, *Orphée et Eurydice*, premiered in Paris on 2 August 1774.
  
  — [Wikipedia: Orfeo ed Euridice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orfeo_ed_Euridice)
- **Gluck's followers.** Salieri is listed among them. — [Wikipedia: Classical period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_period_(music))
- **The piano.**
  - Cristofori invented it in Florence; the first record is a Medici inventory of 1700.
  - The fortepiano reached full popularity from the 1760s. Johannes Zumpe's cheap English square pianos (mid-to-late 1760s) were key to this.
  - Viennese makers Stein and Walter built touch-sensitive instruments, and Mozart owned a Walter.
  - The keyboard range grew from about 4 octaves at invention, to about 5 octaves for Mozart, to about 6½ octaves in Beethoven's last works.
  
  — [Wikipedia: Fortepiano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortepiano)
- **Piano versus harpsichord.** The piano, then the modern piano, replaced the harpsichord as the main and solo keyboard instrument, giving dynamic range and sustained melody. — [Wikipedia: Classical period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_period_(music)); [LibreTexts 13.01](https://human.libretexts.org/Bookshelves/Music/Music_Appreciation/Music_Appreciation_II_(Kuznetsova)/13%3A_The_Classical_Era_Classical_Forms_and_Symphony_J._Haydn/13.01%3A_Overview_of_the_Classical_Era_of_Music)
- **New instruments, new music.** Haydn's Trumpet Concerto (1796) was written for Anton Weidinger's new **keyed trumpet**, which could play chromatically across its whole range, unlike the natural trumpet. — [Wikipedia: Trumpet Concerto (Haydn)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trumpet_Concerto_(Haydn))

### Inferences
- **"Standardisation of the orchestra".** The Mannheim article stresses discipline and effects rather than literally "standardising" the orchestra. It is safer to say "Mannheim set a new standard for orchestral discipline and effects, and the symphony orchestra (strings, paired winds, horns, timpani) took its standard shape in this era."
- **The piano as a theme.** The piano's dynamic control ("forte–piano") is the instrument-level counterpart of the era's taste for contrast and expressive dynamics.

### Gaps
- Gluck's reform manifesto (the preface to *Alceste*, 1767) was not verified in the fetched sources.
- No exact date was found for when the piano "overtook" the harpsichord. It was gradual, from the 1760s to around 1800.

## 3. Composers: the First Viennese School and others

### Takeaway
Haydn, Mozart and Beethoven, all active in Vienna, are the core of "Viennese Classicism". Haydn shaped the symphony and quartet, Mozart perfected opera and the piano concerto, and Beethoven carried the style into Romanticism. Gluck (opera), C. P. E. Bach (expressive keyboard style), Clementi (piano) and Salieri (Viennese opera) are important supporting figures.

### Cited Findings
- **Why "Viennese Classicism".** Haydn, Mozart, Salieri and Beethoven all worked in Vienna, and Schubert was born there. — [LibreTexts 13.01](https://human.libretexts.org/Bookshelves/Music/Music_Appreciation/Music_Appreciation_II_(Kuznetsova)/13%3A_The_Classical_Era_Classical_Forms_and_Symphony_J._Haydn/13.01%3A_Overview_of_the_Classical_Era_of_Music)
- **Haydn (1732–1809), symphony count.** There are **106 symphonies**. **104** carry the numbers Eusebius Mandyczewski assigned in 1908, and two extra works are called "A" and "B". "104 symphonies" therefore refers to the numbered set. — [Wikipedia: List of symphonies by Haydn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_symphonies_by_Joseph_Haydn)
- **Haydn, string quartets.** He wrote **68 authentic** quartets. The older count of 83 included arrangements and spurious works, and the Op. 3 set is probably by Romanus Hoffstetter. — [Wikipedia: List of string quartets by Haydn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_string_quartets_by_Joseph_Haydn)
- **Haydn's "father" titles.**
  - He is called "Father of the Symphony", "Father of the String Quartet" and "Father of Sonata Form".
  - The Haydn scholar James Webster says the quartet title is held with "greater justice" than the symphony title. (Haydn did not invent the symphony; Mannheim and other centres developed it in parallel.)
  
  — [Wikipedia: Joseph Haydn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Haydn); [Wikipedia: Mannheim school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mannheim_school)
- **Haydn's Esterházy career.**
  - He joined the Esterházy court in 1761 as Vice-Kapellmeister and became Kapellmeister in 1766, serving nearly 30 years, largely at the remote palace of Eszterháza in Hungary.
  - On his isolation, he said he was "set apart from the world" and so "had to be original".
  - A 1779 contract change let him sell his works to publishers, which made him Europe's leading composer.
  
  — [Wikipedia: Joseph Haydn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Haydn)
- **Haydn's London visits.** He made two trips, 1791–92 and 1794–95. The first was arranged by the impresario J. P. Salomon. Both were commercially successful and produced the 12 "London" symphonies. — [Wikipedia: Joseph Haydn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Haydn); [Wikipedia: Symphony No. 94](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symphony_No._94_(Haydn))
- **Haydn's links to Mozart and Beethoven.** Haydn and Mozart were friends from about 1784, played quartets together, and Mozart dedicated his six "Haydn" quartets to him. Beethoven studied with Haydn from 1792. — [Wikipedia: Joseph Haydn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Haydn)
- **Mozart (1756–1791).**
  - After quarrels with Archbishop Colloredo of Salzburg he was dismissed in 1781 and settled in Vienna as a **freelance** performer and composer.
  - He earned from his own concerts as piano soloist (1782–85), opera commissions, a part-time imperial chamber-composer post from December 1787 (800 florins a year), and dance-music sales.
  - He died in Vienna on 5 December 1791, aged 35.
  
  — [Wikipedia: Mozart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolfgang_Amadeus_Mozart)
- **Mozart's number of works conflicts between sources.** Wikipedia says "more than 800 works", while LibreTexts says "over 600". The Köchel catalogue numbers run to K. 626, which is probably the source of the "600+" figure. — [Wikipedia: Mozart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolfgang_Amadeus_Mozart); [LibreTexts 15.01](https://human.libretexts.org/Bookshelves/Music/Music_Appreciation/Music_Appreciation_II_(Kuznetsova)/15%3A_Classical_Instrumental_and_Chamber_Music_L._Beethoven/15.01%3A_Review_of_Classical_Era_Music)
- **Mozart's style.** He applied the Classical style to opera and the virtuoso concerto, and absorbed Haydn's ideas and J. S. Bach's counterpoint. — [Wikipedia: Classical period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_period_(music))
- **Beethoven (bapt. 17 December 1770 – d. 26 March 1827).**
  - He moved from Bonn to Vienna in November 1792 and studied with Haydn.
  - He lived on aristocratic patrons, public concerts and publishers.
  - In 1808 Archduke Rudolf, Prince Kinsky and Prince Lobkowitz pledged him 4,000 florins a year to stay in Vienna (only partly paid).
  - His hearing loss began around 1798.
  - His career is divided into three periods: early (Haydn/Mozart style), middle "heroic" (1802–1812) and late (1812–1827).
  - He was "a key driver of the transition from Classical to Romantic music".
  
  — [Wikipedia: Beethoven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig_van_Beethoven)
- **Is Beethoven Classical or Romantic?** He is regarded either as a Romantic composer or as a transitional one. Wikipedia calls his *Eroica* (Symphony No. 3) "the first symphony of the Romantic era". — [LibreTexts 13.01](https://human.libretexts.org/Bookshelves/Music/Music_Appreciation/Music_Appreciation_II_(Kuznetsova)/13%3A_The_Classical_Era_Classical_Forms_and_Symphony_J._Haydn/13.01%3A_Overview_of_the_Classical_Era_of_Music); [Wikipedia: Classical period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_period_(music))
- **Clementi.** A virtuoso pianist and influential sonata composer based in London, who took part in a keyboard contest with Mozart before the Emperor. — [Wikipedia: Classical period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_period_(music))
- **C. P. E. Bach.** The leader of *Empfindsamkeit*. — [Wikipedia: Classical period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_period_(music))

### Inferences
- **For narration.** "Haydn built the forms, Mozart perfected them, Beethoven broke them open" is a common teaching simplification. It is fair as a summary, but should not be presented as a quotation.

### Gaps
- Salieri's and Clementi's dates and key works were not separately verified; use their own biographies if they are named in the script.
- No separate verification was done for C. P. E. Bach's dates and posts.

## 4. Where music was performed and used

### Takeaway
Music moved from serving courts and churches toward a market. Aristocratic courts (such as Esterházy) and the church still employed musicians. Opera houses (the Vienna Burgtheater, Prague's Estates Theatre), ticketed public and subscription concerts (Paris, London), salons, home music-making and music publishing all grew, allowing Mozart and Beethoven to work as freelancers.

### Cited Findings
- **Court patronage.** Haydn was employed by the Esterházy family (1761 to about 1790) at Eszterháza. — [Wikipedia: Joseph Haydn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Haydn)
- **Shifting patronage.** Patronage moved from the church and courts to the nobility, and later to public concerts. "The nobility became the primary patrons of instrumental music," while public taste increasingly favoured comic opera. Court ensembles were often small and of fixed ability, so parts were often kept simple and works had to be playable after one rehearsal. — [Wikipedia: Classical period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_period_(music)); [LibreTexts 13.01](https://human.libretexts.org/Bookshelves/Music/Music_Appreciation/Music_Appreciation_II_(Kuznetsova)/13%3A_The_Classical_Era_Classical_Forms_and_Symphony_J._Haydn/13.01%3A_Overview_of_the_Classical_Era_of_Music)
- **Vienna Burgtheater.** It hosted the premiere of Gluck's *Orfeo ed Euridice* (5 October 1762) and of Haydn's Trumpet Concerto (28 March 1800). — [Wikipedia: Orfeo ed Euridice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orfeo_ed_Euridice); [Wikipedia: Trumpet Concerto (Haydn)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trumpet_Concerto_(Haydn))
- **Prague.**
  - Mozart's *Don Giovanni* (K. 527, libretto by Lorenzo Da Ponte) premiered on **29 October 1787** at the National Theatre of Bohemia, now the **Estates Theatre**, Prague.
  - It was commissioned after the success of Mozart's Prague visit in January–February 1787. (That visit followed the Prague success of *Figaro*, but the fetched article does not say *Figaro* caused the commission.)
  - The Vienna premiere was on 7 May 1788.
  
  — [Wikipedia: Don Giovanni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_Giovanni)
- **Theater an der Wien.** It staged the 22 December 1808 all-Beethoven concert (Symphony No. 5 premiere, conducted by Beethoven), a public benefit concert. — [Wikipedia: Symphony No. 5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symphony_No._5_(Beethoven))
- **Public concerts, Paris.** The **Concert Spirituel**, founded by Anne Danican Philidor in **1725**, was a public, ticketed series that lasted until 1790 and served as a model for other countries. — [Interlude: The Concert Spirituel](https://interlude.hk/the-concert-spirituel-the-first-public-concert-series/)
- **Public concerts, London.**
  - J. C. Bach and C. F. Abel ran subscription concerts from the mid-1760s (sources give 1764 or 1765). They were expensive, so audiences were largely upper-class.
  - The Hanover Square Rooms (opened 1775) held about 900 people.
  - Salomon's concerts (1791–95) brought Haydn to London. — [Wikipedia: Professional Concerts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professional_Concerts); [Interlude](https://interlude.hk/when-was-the-first-public-classical-music-concert/)
  - Haydn's Symphony No. 94 premiered at the Hanover Square Rooms on 23 March 1792, with Haydn directing from the fortepiano. — [Wikipedia: Symphony No. 94](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symphony_No._94_(Haydn))
- **Broader audiences.** Concert audiences drew on a wider social range only from the early 19th century. — [Britannica: Concert](https://www.britannica.com/art/concert) (via search summary; page not fetched directly)
- **Private salons.** The aristocratic salon is a documented venue: a poor performance of Mozart's Symphony No. 40 took place at Baron van Swieten's home. — [Wikipedia: Symphony No. 40](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symphony_No._40_(Mozart))
- **Freelancing.** Mozart went freelance in Vienna after 1781, and Beethoven lived on patrons, concerts and publishers. — [Wikipedia: Mozart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolfgang_Amadeus_Mozart); [Wikipedia: Beethoven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig_van_Beethoven)
- **Publishing.**
  - Mozart's K. 331 sonata was published by Artaria (Vienna) in 1784.
  - Beethoven's "Moonlight" Sonata was published by Cappi (Vienna) in 1802.
  - Haydn's 1779 contract let him sell his works to publishers.
  
  — [Wikipedia: Piano Sonata No. 11](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piano_Sonata_No._11_(Mozart)); [Wikipedia: Piano Sonata No. 14](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piano_Sonata_No._14_(Beethoven)); [Wikipedia: Joseph Haydn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Haydn)
- **Church music.** Mozart's Requiem K. 626 (1791) was commissioned anonymously by Count Franz von Walsegg for his late wife. — [Wikipedia: Requiem (Mozart)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Requiem_(Mozart))

### Inferences
- **Keyboard music for amateurs.** Cheap square pianos from the 1760s, publishers such as Artaria and Cappi, and published keyboard sonatas together point to a growing market of middle-class amateurs playing at home.
- **Market versus court.** Haydn's career shows the shift in one life: a court servant at Eszterháza became a market celebrity in London.

### Gaps
- No good source was fetched on domestic music-making or the bourgeois amateur market as such. The point above is inferred from the publishing and piano facts.
- The venue of *Le nozze di Figaro*'s 1786 Vienna premiere (commonly said to be the Burgtheater) was not verified in fetched text.

## 5. Five recommended pieces for 10–20 second excerpts (verified catalogue data)

### Takeaway
Five well-known pieces, verified and spread across the three main composers, are listed below, followed by backups. Some dates commonly quoted need adjustment: K. 331's year is debated, Beethoven's Fifth took 1804–1808, and *Für Elise* was written in 1810 but published only in 1867.

### Cited Findings
**Recommended five:**
1. **W. A. Mozart – *Eine kleine Nachtmusik* (Serenade No. 13 in G major), K. 525.** Completed in Vienna on **10 August 1787** and published only about 1827 (André, Offenbach). It is scored for two violins, viola, cello and double bass, and is often played by a string orchestra. Four movements survive; a fifth (a minuet) is lost. The 1st movement Allegro (sonata form) is the classic excerpt. — [Wikipedia: Eine kleine Nachtmusik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eine_kleine_Nachtmusik)
2. **W. A. Mozart – Symphony No. 40 in G minor, K. 550.** Entered in Mozart's catalogue on **25 July 1788**, written in a few weeks alongside Nos. 39 and 41. Its premiere date is unknown. — [Wikipedia: Symphony No. 40](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symphony_No._40_(Mozart))
3. **Joseph Haydn – Symphony No. 94 in G major "Surprise", Hob. I:94.** Composed in **1791** in London and premiered on **23 March 1792** at the Hanover Square Rooms. The "surprise" is a sudden loud chord in bar 16 of the quiet 2nd movement (Andante). This makes it an ideal excerpt, since the joke is audible within about 20 seconds. — [Wikipedia: Symphony No. 94](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symphony_No._94_(Haydn))
4. **L. van Beethoven – Symphony No. 5 in C minor, Op. 67.** Composed **1804–1808** and premiered on **22 December 1808** at the Theater an der Wien, conducted by Beethoven. It is dedicated to Prince Lobkowitz and Count Razumovsky. E. T. A. Hoffmann praised it as an "indescribably profound, magnificent symphony". — [Wikipedia: Symphony No. 5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symphony_No._5_(Beethoven))
5. **Mozart – "Rondo alla Turca"** (Mozart's own heading "Alla turca"), the 3rd movement of **Piano Sonata No. 11 in A major, K. 331 (K. 300i)**. Wikipedia gives **1783**; it was published by Artaria in 1784. — [Wikipedia: Piano Sonata No. 11](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piano_Sonata_No._11_(Mozart))
   - *Alternative to No. 5:* **Beethoven – Piano Sonata No. 14 in C♯ minor, Op. 27 No. 2 "Moonlight"** (*Sonata quasi una fantasia*). Composed in **1801**, published by Cappi in Vienna in 1802, and dedicated to Countess Giulietta Guicciardi. The "Moonlight" nickname came from the critic Ludwig Rellstab in 1824, after Beethoven's lifetime. — [Wikipedia: Piano Sonata No. 14](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piano_Sonata_No._14_(Beethoven))

**Backups (also verified):**
- **Beethoven – *Für Elise* (Bagatelle No. 25 in A minor), WoO 59.** Dated **27 April 1810** and published by Ludwig Nohl in **1867**, 40 years after Beethoven's death. Who "Elise" was is unknown; candidates are Therese Malfatti, Elisabeth Röckel and Elise Barensfeld. — [Wikipedia: Für Elise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F%C3%BCr_Elise)
- **Haydn – Trumpet Concerto in E-flat major, Hob. VIIe:1.** Composed in **1796** and premiered on 28 March 1800 at the Burgtheater, written for Weidinger's keyed trumpet. — [Wikipedia: Trumpet Concerto (Haydn)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trumpet_Concerto_(Haydn))
- **Haydn – String Quartet in C major, Op. 76 No. 3 "Emperor", Hob. III:77.** Composed **1797** (the Op. 76 set dates to 1797–98) and dedicated to Count Joseph Erdődy. Its 2nd movement varies Haydn's anthem "Gott erhalte Franz den Kaiser", whose melody became the Austro-Hungarian anthem and later the German national anthem (*Deutschlandlied*). — [Wikipedia: String Quartets, Op. 76](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/String_Quartets,_Op._76_(Haydn))
- **Mozart – Requiem in D minor, K. 626, "Lacrimosa".** Composed in 1791 and left unfinished. **Mozart wrote only the first 8 bars** of the Lacrimosa; Süssmayr completed it. If it is used, say "Mozart's last work, completed by his pupil Süssmayr". — [Wikipedia: Requiem (Mozart)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Requiem_(Mozart))

### Inferences
- **Spread.** The recommended five cover a serenade, two symphonies (one by each of Mozart and Haydn), a Beethoven symphony and a piano piece, and span 1783–1808. They show the genres, the orchestra, the piano and the Classical-to-Romantic arc.
- **Best excerpts.** The Haydn "Surprise" (dynamic contrast) and the Beethoven Fifth's opening motif are the most effective 10–20 second clips for making an argument.
- **Für Elise caveat.** It is famous, but stylistically and chronologically it is late (1810, published 1867). It is fine as Beethoven's piano music, but weaker as the "Classical style" example.

### Gaps

## 6. Is Beethoven's Symphony No. 9 (1824) Classical or Romantic?

### Takeaway
It is best described as transitional or early Romantic, not "Classical". It postdates the conventional 1820 end. Its use of chorus and soloists was a first for a major composer's symphony, and Wikipedia's infobox labels it "Classical-Romantic (transitional)". Beethoven himself is widely treated as the bridge between the two eras.

### Cited Findings
- **Facts.** Symphony No. 9 in D minor, Op. 125, was composed 1822–1824 (commissioned by the Philharmonic Society of London in 1817) and premiered on **7 May 1824** at the Theater am Kärntnertor, Vienna. Its text is Schiller's "An die Freude". "The Ninth was the first example of a major composer scoring vocal parts in a symphony." — [Wikipedia: Symphony No. 9](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symphony_No._9_(Beethoven))
- **Period label.** The infobox reads "Classical-Romantic (transitional)". — [Wikipedia: Symphony No. 9](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symphony_No._9_(Beethoven))
- **Anthem of Europe.** The Council of Europe adopted the theme in 1972 and the European Communities (now the EU) in 1985. — [Wikipedia: Symphony No. 9](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symphony_No._9_(Beethoven))
- **Beethoven's position.** His late period was 1812–1827, and he was a "key driver of the transition from Classical to Romantic music". Some writers call the *Eroica* (1803–04) the first Romantic symphony. — [Wikipedia: Beethoven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig_van_Beethoven); [Wikipedia: Classical period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_period_(music))

- **How to say it.** "Beethoven's Ninth (1824) stands at the threshold of Romanticism: Classical in its four-movement frame, Romantic in its scale and its choral finale." The second half is interpretive framing.

## 7. Why the Classical era is a key milestone (material for the argument)

### Takeaway
The era created the forms, ensembles and instruments that still define "classical music" concerts today: the symphony, string quartet, solo concerto, the standard orchestra and the piano. It also moved composers from servants of courts to independent artists selling to a paying public, making it the bridge from the Baroque to the Romantic and modern concert world.

### Cited Findings
- **Forms that last.** Sonata form became the dominant structure, and the symphony, quartet and solo concerto became the core genres. — [Wikipedia: Classical period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_period_(music))
- **The ensembles.** The standard orchestra took shape: standardised strings and a self-contained wind section, with no continuo. — [Wikipedia: Classical period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_period_(music))
- **The instrument.** The piano replaced the harpsichord. — [Wikipedia: Fortepiano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortepiano)
- **A new economy.**
  - Public ticketed and subscription concerts appeared (Paris 1725, London 1760s–90s).
  - Haydn's market success came through publishing and his London concerts.
  - Mozart (from 1781) and Beethoven (from 1792) worked as freelancers in Vienna.
  
  — [Interlude](https://interlude.hk/the-concert-spirituel-the-first-public-concert-series/); [Wikipedia: Joseph Haydn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Haydn); [Wikipedia: Mozart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolfgang_Amadeus_Mozart); [Wikipedia: Beethoven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig_van_Beethoven)
- **Opera reform.** Gluck's "noble simplicity" put drama ahead of vocal display. — [Wikipedia: Orfeo ed Euridice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orfeo_ed_Euridice)
- **Legacy.** Beethoven's Fifth influenced Brahms, Tchaikovsky, Bruckner, Mahler and Berlioz. — [Wikipedia: Symphony No. 5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symphony_No._5_(Beethoven))

### Inferences
- **A three-part argument for the narration:**
  1. **Form.** Music gained clear, rational architecture (sonata form, the four-movement symphony) that composers have used ever since.
  2. **Sound.** The modern orchestra and the piano arrived.
  3. **Society.** Music moved from palace and church to the paying public and the printed score, and the composer became an independent artist.
- **"Classical music".** The everyday meaning of "classical music" partly comes from this era's canon. This is an interpretive claim, so phrase it as an opinion.
