# History of Sound Recording and the Digitization of Music (1857 to streaming), status as of Oct 2026

Research notes for a 1.5–2 minute narrated school segment. All figures carry the date they refer to. Items I could not verify with a source in this session are in the **Gaps** sections, with the commonly cited value marked "unverified".

---

## 1. Analog era: from the phonautograph to the Walkman

### Takeaway
Sound was first *written down* in 1857–1860 (Scott de Martinville), first *played back* in 1877 (Edison), and turned into a mass product by Berliner's flat disc, which was patented in 1887. Tape (1935), the LP and the 45 (1948–49), the cassette (1963) and the Walkman (1979) then made music editable, longer, cheaper and portable.

### Cited Findings
**Phonautograph (Scott de Martinville)**
- Édouard-Léon Scott de Martinville came up with the idea in 1853 while editing a physiology textbook. He developed and experimented with it from 1857 to 1860. — [Library of Congress, Now See Hear! blog (2021)](https://blogs.loc.gov/now-see-hear/2021/08/from-the-recording-registry-phonautograms-c-1853-61/)
- His recording of "Au Clair de la Lune" dates from **9 April 1860**. It was made on a phonautograph, a machine built to record sound *visually* (as a line on paper) with no way to play it back. — [Science News](https://www.sciencenews.org/?p=4700); [LoC blog](https://blogs.loc.gov/now-see-hear/2021/08/from-the-recording-registry-phonautograms-c-1853-61/)
- It was first heard in **2008**. Lawrence Berkeley Lab scientists made high-resolution scans of the paper and played them with optical imaging and a "virtual stylus". — [Science News](https://www.sciencenews.org/?p=4700); [The Day, 27 Mar 2008](https://www.theday.com/dayarc/20080327/researchers-play-1860-tune-recorded-before-edison/)
- The 1860 recording is about 28 years older than Edison's 1888 Handel recording, which until then was thought to be the oldest playable recording. — [Smithsonian Magazine](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/smithsonian-institution/from-the-collections-sound-recordings-heard-for-the-first-time-6989525/)
- The Library of Congress added the phonautograms to its National Recording Registry in **2010**. — [LoC blog](https://blogs.loc.gov/now-see-hear/2021/08/from-the-recording-registry-phonautograms-c-1853-61/)

**Edison phonograph (1877)**
- Edison's 1877 phonograph cut grooves into tinfoil on a cylinder. It was the first machine that could both record and play back sound. In the first test he recited "Mary had a little lamb". — [Birthplace of Country Music Museum](https://birthplaceofcountrymusic.org/thomas-edison-from-mary-had-a-little-lamb-to-recorded-music/); [HistoryofInformation](https://www.historyofinformation.com/detail.php?id=509)
- He showed the machine at the *Scientific American* offices in New York on **7 Dec 1877** and filed for a patent on **24 Dec 1877**. US Patent **200,521** was granted on **19 Feb 1878**. The often-quoted invention date of "12 August 1877" is disputed by historians. — [HistoryofInformation](https://www.historyofinformation.com/detail.php?id=509)

**Berliner gramophone and the flat disc (1887)**
- Emile Berliner received US patent **372,786** for the Gramophone on **8 Nov 1887**. He used a flat disc with a lateral (side-to-side) groove instead of Edison's cylinder. — [ETHW – Gramophone](https://ethw.org/Gramophone); [Canadian Communications Foundation](https://broadcasting-history.ca/?p=9417)
- The Library of Congress describes Berliner, who worked in Washington, D.C., as creating "a new medium for sound recording in the late 1880s". — [LoC American Treasures (archived)](https://webarchive.library.unt.edu/eot2008/20090114201739/http:/www.loc.gov/exhibits/treasures/trr017.html)

**Magnetic tape: AEG Magnetophon (1935)**
- AEG showed the Magnetophon K1 tape recorder, which used low-cost magnetic tape, at the **Berlin Radio Show in August 1935**. A planned 1934 debut was cancelled two days before that show. — [Computer History Museum](https://www.computerhistory.org/storageengine/audio-recorder-uses-low-cost-magnetic-tape/)

**LP and 45 (1948–49)**
- Columbia Records developed the 33⅓ rpm long-playing record (LP) and introduced it in **1948**. RCA Victor introduced the 7-inch 45 rpm single in **1949**. One source dates the first 45 to February 1949. — [45-rpm.org.uk – History of the Gramophone Record](https://45-rpm.org.uk/history.html)

**Compact cassette (Philips, 1963)**
- In **August 1963** Philips introduced its first compact cassette recorder, the EL 3300, at the Funkausstellung (radio exhibition) in Berlin. — [Philips official media library](https://www.philips.com/a-w/about/news/media-library/20190101-First-Philips-cassette-recorder-1963.html). Other sources give 28 or 30 August 1963 as the exact day. — [The Register, 2013](https://www.theregister.com/Print/2013/08/30/50_years_of_the_compact_cassette/)
- Lou Ottens (Philips) invented the cassette. He died aged 94. — [What Hi-Fi](https://www.whathifi.com/news/compact-cassette-tape-inventor-lou-ottens-has-died-aged-94)

**Sony Walkman (1979)**
- The first personal stereo cassette player, the Sony Walkman (TPS-L2), went on sale on **1 July 1979**. Journalists saw it first on 22 June 1979. Only about **3,000 units** sold in July 1979, its first month. — [HISTORY.com](https://www.history.com/this-day-in-history/july-1/the-first-sony-walkman-goes-on-sale); [Mental Floss](https://www.mentalfloss.com/article/65620/july-1-1979-sonys-walkman-changes-recorded-history)

### Inferences
- The story runs in four steps: *write sound down* (1860), *play it back* (1877), *mass-produce it* (1887 flat disc that could be stamped out in copies), *carry it with you* (1963 cassette, 1979 Walkman). Digital technology later repeated the same steps (CD, MP3, iPod, phone streaming).

### Gaps
- **Singer of the 1860 phonautogram:** in 2008 it was played as a young woman or girl. My background knowledge (unverified here) is that the First Sounds team re-checked the speed in 2009 and concluded it is a man, probably Scott himself, singing slowly. Check firstsounds.org before stating who sings.
- **Phonautograph patent:** usually given as French patent of **25 March 1857**. Unverified in this session; the LoC page only says "1857–1860".
- **Shellac 78 rpm:** not verified. Commonly said: shellac discs at about 78 rpm were the standard from around 1900 until the 1950s, with 78 rpm becoming standard around 1925.
- **Radio (1920s):** not verified. Commonly cited: KDKA Pittsburgh began the first commercial broadcast on 2 Nov 1920; BBC (British Broadcasting Company) began in 1922.
- **Les Paul / multitrack recording:** not verified. Commonly cited: Les Paul's sound-on-sound overdubbing in the late 1940s; Ampex 8-track recorder ("Octopus") 1957.
- **Stereo records 1958:** not verified. Commonly cited: the first mass-produced stereo LPs were sold in 1958 (Westrex 45/45 system).

## 2. The digital turn: CD, MIDI, MP3, Napster, iPod, iTunes, YouTube, Spotify, Apple Music

### Takeaway
The first CDs were pressed in Germany in August 1982 and went on sale in Japan in October 1982. Fraunhofer's MP3, named on 14 July 1995, then cut file sizes enough for music to move over the internet. Napster (1999) showed demand, iPod and iTunes (2001/2003) made digital sales legal and simple, and streaming (Spotify 2008, Apple Music 2015) replaced owning music with access to it.

### Cited Findings
**Compact Disc (Sony + Philips)**
- **First CD manufactured:** on **17 Aug 1982** at the Philips/PolyGram plant in **Langenhagen, near Hanover, West Germany**. ABBA's *The Visitors* (1981) is widely named as the first CD pressed there. — [This Day in Tech History](https://thisdayintechhistory.com/08/17/the-first-music-cd); [Vintage Digital](https://www.vintagedigital.com.au/?p=191180); [Sound & Vision](https://www.soundandvision.com/content/flashback-1982-abba-cd-world’s-first)
- **The "first CD" debate:** some sources say the very first *test* CD from the plant held Richard Strauss's *Alpine Symphony* conducted by Herbert von Karajan. — [TNT-Audio](https://www.tnt-audio.com/edcorner/october12.html); [Scribd document "First"](https://www.scribd.com/doc/305213419/First). Others name Claudio Arrau's Chopin waltzes (Philips 400 025-2) as the first commercial CD pressed on 17 Aug 1982, with ABBA as the first *pop* CD. — [Scribd](https://www.scribd.com/doc/305213419/First); [Octane/Magzter](https://www.magzter.com/ja/stories/automotive/Octane/COMPACT-DISC). **Safe wording:** "the first CDs were pressed in Germany in August 1982; ABBA's *The Visitors* is often called the first."
- **First commercial sale:** on **1 Oct 1982** in Japan, together with Sony's **CDP-101** player. Billy Joel's *52nd Street* (CBS/Sony) is often called "the first CD released". It was one of about **50 titles** released that day and simply had the first catalogue number. — [Hypebot – Who released the first ever CD](https://www.hypebot.com/who-released-the-first-ever-cd/); [American Songwriter](https://americansongwriter.com/did-you-know-billy-joel-was-the-first-artist-released-on-cd-in-1982); [The Next Web, 1 Oct 2012](https://thenextweb.com/news/the-first-commerically-available-cd-album-player-released-30-years-ago-today?awesm=tnw.to_a5PH)
- One German source says sales began in November and only in Japan, which conflicts with the 1 October date. — [PC-Welt](https://www.pcwelt.de/?p=1111582). Most sources give 1 Oct 1982.

**MIDI (1983)**
- MIDI was first shown publicly at the **Winter NAMM Show, January 1983**, by connecting a Sequential Circuits Prophet-600 to a Roland Jupiter-6. The MIDI 1.0 Specification was published in **August 1983**. Wikipedia gives 1 Feb 1983 as the introduction date, so sources differ. — [Mix Online – 1983 Dave Smith/Sequential Circuits MIDI](https://www.mixonline.com/technology/1983-dave-smith-sequential-circuits-midi-specification-383642); [MIDI Association](https://midi.org/historical-early-midi-documents-uncovered); [Wikipedia – MIDI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIDI)

**MP3 (Fraunhofer IIS, Erlangen)**
- "The development of mp3 started in the late 80s at Fraunhofer IIS", building on earlier work at the University of Erlangen-Nuremberg. — [Fraunhofer IIS](https://www.iis.fraunhofer.de/en/ff/amm/consumer-electronics/mp3.html)
- Karlheinz Brandenburg made audio compression, a problem many thought could not be solved, the subject of his doctoral thesis in Erlangen. MP3 (MPEG-1 Audio Layer III) builds on the Thomson/Fraunhofer ASPEC method and the MDCT transform. — [CIO.de](https://www.cio.de/article/4020008/30-jahre-mp3-die-musikwelt-war-danach-keine-scheibe-mehr.html); [Heise, "30 years of MP3"](https://heise.de/-10485563)
- **14 July 1995:** after an internal Fraunhofer poll, an email declared ".mp3" the file extension for ISO MPEG Audio Layer 3. It won unanimously. — [Heise](https://heise.de/-10485563); [Gearnews, "30 years of MP3"](https://www.gearnews.com/30-years-of-mp3-tech/)
- The mp3 licensing programme run by Technicolor and Fraunhofer ended on **23 April 2017**. — [Fraunhofer IIS](https://www.iis.fraunhofer.de/en/ff/amm/consumer-electronics/mp3.html)

**Napster, iPod, iTunes, YouTube, Spotify, Apple Music**
- **Napster:** Shawn Fanning released it in **June 1999**. A court order shut it down in **July 2001**. — [Yahoo News – 25 years since Napster](https://ca.news.yahoo.com/25-years-since-napster-launched-100154339.html); [AOL – Napster forced to shut down](https://www.aol.com/articles/napster-forced-shut-down-court-195007783.html)
- **iPod 2001; iTunes Store 2003**, which sold **1 million downloads in its first 3 days**. **YouTube and Pandora 2005. Spotify launched in 2008** in selected European countries and **in the US in 2011. Apple Music and the YouTube Music app 2015. Amazon Music Unlimited 2016.** — [DiMA Innovation Timeline](https://dima.org/innovation-timeline/)
- Streams have counted toward the Billboard Hot 100 since **2007**. "Spotify Wrapped" launched in **2016**. — [DiMA Innovation Timeline](https://dima.org/innovation-timeline/)

### Inferences
- The CD was the first *digital* consumer music format. Music became numbers, which later made copying with no loss of quality, and the MP3, possible: a CD could be "ripped" and compressed.
- The order Napster (illegal) → iTunes (legal paid downloads) → Spotify (legal access to everything) shows how the industry first fought digitization and then built its business on it.

### Gaps
- **CD technical specs (44.1 kHz / 16-bit / 12 cm / ~74 min):** not verified with a source in this session, because sony.com blocked fetching. These are the standard Red Book values in my background knowledge; check Sony's or Philips' official CD history before stating them.
- **Exact days, unverified here:** iPod announced 23 Oct 2001; iTunes Music Store opened 28 Apr 2003; YouTube's first video "Me at the zoo" uploaded 23 Apr 2005; Spotify public launch 7 Oct 2008 (Stockholm); Apple Music launched 30 Jun 2015. DiMA confirms only the years.
- **Digital audio workstations (DAWs):** no source collected. Commonly cited examples: Pro Tools (Digidesign) in 1991; GarageBand bundled free on Macs from 2004.

## 3. Current state of the market (latest figures, 2025 data published in 2026)

### Takeaway
In 2025, global recorded-music revenue grew **6.4% to US$31.7 billion**. Streaming made up **69.6%** of it, and there were **837 million** paid subscription accounts (IFPI, published 18 Mar 2026). Vinyl is the physical format that keeps growing: in the US it passed **$1 billion** in 2025 for the first time since 1983 and outsells CDs by about 3 to 1 in revenue. In Q3 2026 Spotify had **788 million users** and **306 million paying subscribers**.

### Cited Findings
**IFPI Global Music Report 2026 (published 18 March 2026, covers calendar year 2025)**
- Global recorded-music revenue was **US$31.7 billion, up 6.4%**, the **11th year of growth in a row** (2024 growth was 4.7%). — [IFPI press release, 18 Mar 2026](https://www.ifpi.org/global-music-report-2026-global-recorded-music-revenues-grow-6-4-as-record-companies-drive-innovation/); [Nairametrics](https://nairametrics.com/2026/03/19/global-recorded-music-revenue-hits-31-7-billion-in-2025-report/)
- Total streaming revenue passed **US$22 billion**, which is **69.6%** of global income. — [IFPI](https://www.ifpi.org/global-music-report-2026-global-recorded-music-revenues-grow-6-4-as-record-companies-drive-innovation/)
- Paid subscription streaming grew **+8.8%** and made up **52.4%** of total revenue. — [IFPI](https://www.ifpi.org/global-music-report-2026-global-recorded-music-revenues-grow-6-4-as-record-companies-drive-innovation/)
- Paid subscription accounts reached **837 million** (end of 2025), up from **752 million** a year earlier. — [IFPI](https://www.ifpi.org/global-music-report-2026-global-recorded-music-revenues-grow-6-4-as-record-companies-drive-innovation/); [Pulse Kenya](https://www.pulse.co.ke/story/837-million-subscribers-later-how-streaming-is-reshaping-music-2026031913332958532)
- Physical revenue rose **+8.0%**. Vinyl rose **+13.7%**, its **19th year of growth in a row**. — [IFPI](https://www.ifpi.org/global-music-report-2026-global-recorded-music-revenues-grow-6-4-as-record-companies-drive-innovation/)
- Latin America was the fastest-growing region at **+17.1%**. Europe grew **+5.6%** and has 30.4% of global revenue. — [IFPI](https://www.ifpi.org/global-music-report-2026-global-recorded-music-revenues-grow-6-4-as-record-companies-drive-innovation/). One secondary source gives Latin America as +22.5%; IFPI's own figure is 17.1%.
- **Milestone:** "Global recorded music revenues top $30 billion for the first time since 1999", in a secondary headline. — [That Eric Alper](https://www.thatericalper.com/?p=228599). DiMA also notes that 2025 revenue topped $30 billion. — [DiMA](https://dima.org/innovation-timeline/). *Caution:* the "since 1999" comparison is in nominal dollars, not adjusted for inflation, and I did not confirm it on IFPI's own page.

**RIAA (USA), 2025 Year-End Report (published 16 March 2026)**
- US recorded-music revenue hit a record **US$11.5 billion** (+3.1%). — [Music Week](https://www.musicweek.com/labels/read/riaa-us-revenue-hits-record-high-as-vinyl-sales-pass-1-billion/093770); [Billboard](https://billboard.com/pro/riaa-2025-music-report-revenue-streaming-vinyl)
- Streaming was **$9.5 billion, 82%** of US revenue. There were **106.5 million** paid subscriptions. — [Music Week](https://www.musicweek.com/labels/read/riaa-us-revenue-hits-record-high-as-vinyl-sales-pass-1-billion/093770)
- Vinyl revenue was **$1.04 billion (+9.3%)** from **46.8 million units**. This is the **first time over $1 billion since 1983** and the 19th year of growth. — [Music Week](https://www.musicweek.com/labels/read/riaa-us-revenue-hits-record-high-as-vinyl-sales-pass-1-billion/093770); [Rolling Stone India](https://rollingstoneindia.com/taylor-swift-helps-u-s-vinyl-sales-surpass-1-billion-for-first-time-since-1983/amp)
- CD revenue was **$312.4 million (−11.6%)** from **29.5 million units**. Vinyl earned more than **3 times** CD revenue and also sold more units (46.8M vs 29.5M). — [Billboard](https://billboard.com/pro/riaa-2025-music-report-revenue-streaming-vinyl); [Music Week](https://www.musicweek.com/labels/read/riaa-us-revenue-hits-record-high-as-vinyl-sales-pass-1-billion/093770). *Conflict:* another outlet gives −11.6% for CD *units* and −7.8% for *revenue*.
- Downloads keep falling ("Streams up; downloads down"). — [RAIN News](https://rainnews.com/5streams-up-downloads-down-riaa/)

**Spotify (latest: Q3 2026, reported 22 Oct 2026)**
- **788 million** monthly active users (+11% year on year) and **306 million** Premium subscribers (+9%). Revenue was about **€4.97 billion** (+16%). Spotify's Q4 2026 forecast is 818M MAU and 316M subscribers. — [Hypebot, 22 Oct 2026](https://www.hypebot.com/spotify-q3-2026-788m-users-and-306m-subscribers/)
- Spotify passed **300 million** paid subscribers by Q2 2026 (reported in August 2026). — [Express Tribune](https://tribune.com.pk/story/2622030/spotify-tops-300-million-paid-subscribers-despite-earnings-miss)

### Inferences
- From 1999 to 2025 the industry went through the Napster crash and fell for years. Streaming brought it back to nominal revenue above US$30 billion. Streaming did not kill the music business; it became its foundation (about 70% of revenue).
- Vinyl's comeback goes together with digitization rather than against it. People stream for convenience and buy records as collector or fan objects.

### Gaps
- I did not collect a source for when vinyl first outsold CDs in the US. My background knowledge (unverified here) is that RIAA reported vinyl revenue passing CD revenue in **2020** (H1 2020 and full year), and vinyl *units* passing CD units in **2022** (41M vs 33M). Check RIAA's 2020 and 2022 reports.
- I did not verify the date of Spotify's latest figures on Spotify Investor Relations (investors.spotify.com); they come from trade press (Hypebot).

## 4. How digitization changed who makes music and how people listen; why it is a turning point

### Takeaway
Digitization moved music from scarce physical objects (records, CDs) to unlimited data. Making music moved from expensive studios to laptops (MIDI 1983, DAWs, GarageBand), and listening moved from owning albums to having almost any song on a phone.

### Cited Findings
- **Listening:** streaming now brings in about 70% of world recorded-music revenue (US$22bn+ of $31.7bn in 2025), and **837 million** people or accounts pay for subscriptions. — [IFPI, 18 Mar 2026](https://www.ifpi.org/global-music-report-2026-global-recorded-music-revenues-grow-6-4-as-record-companies-drive-innovation/)
- **Portability:** the Walkman (1979) first made personal, mobile listening possible. — [HISTORY.com](https://www.history.com/this-day-in-history/july-1/the-first-sony-walkman-goes-on-sale). The iPod (2001), the iTunes Store (2003, 1 million downloads in 3 days) and the iPhone App Store (2008) carried this into the digital era. — [DiMA](https://dima.org/innovation-timeline/)
- **Creation:** MIDI (1983) let synthesizers and computers from different makers talk to each other, which is the basis of computer-based music production. — [Mix Online](https://www.mixonline.com/technology/1983-dave-smith-sequential-circuits-midi-specification-383642)
- **Distribution and discovery:** Spotify's algorithmic playlists "Discover Weekly" and "New Music Friday" (2015) and AI DJ (2023). — [DiMA](https://dima.org/innovation-timeline/). Spotify programmes now pay creators in many markets, for example the Partner Program expanding to more than 35 new markets (2026). — [Hypebot](https://www.hypebot.com/spotify-q3-2026-788m-users-and-306m-subscribers/)
- **New issues:** the IFPI 2026 report highlights AI licensing and streaming fraud as major themes. — [IFPI](https://www.ifpi.org/global-music-report-2026-global-recorded-music-revenues-grow-6-4-as-record-companies-drive-innovation/)

### Inferences (argument for the presentation: why this is a key turning point)
1. **Music stopped being an object and became data.** For about 120 years (1877 to the 1990s), owning music meant owning a cylinder, disc or tape. The CD (1982) turned sound into numbers, the MP3 (1995) made those numbers small enough for the internet, and streaming removed the need to own anything.
2. **Access went from scarce to almost unlimited.** A listener used to own a shelf of albums; now one subscription opens tens of millions of songs, and 837 million accounts pay for this (2025).
3. **Anyone can make music.** Thanks to MIDI, DAWs and cheap software, a "bedroom producer" with a laptop can record, release and reach listeners worldwide without a record label or a costly studio.
4. **It saved and rebuilt the industry.** After the Napster shock, the business fell for years. Streaming led it to 11 years of growth in a row, to US$31.7 billion in 2025.
5. **The analog past did not disappear.** Vinyl grew for 19 years in a row and passed $1bn in the US in 2025, the first time since 1983, showing that digitization changed the *meaning* of physical formats.

### Gaps
- No hard statistics were collected on the number of home or bedroom producers or tracks uploaded per day. A figure often quoted in industry press is "over 100,000 tracks uploaded to streaming services daily" (Luminate/MRC, around 2023), but it is **unverified here**.
- No source collected for specific bedroom-produced hits. Commonly cited examples: Billie Eilish and Finneas recorded *When We All Fall Asleep, Where Do We Go?* (2019) in a bedroom; Lil Nas X's "Old Town Road" used a beat bought online for about $30. Both are unverified here.

### Suggested ~1.5–2 min narration skeleton (built only from the verified facts above)
1860: Scott de Martinville "writes" a voice onto paper and records "Au Clair de la Lune", first heard in 2008 → 1877: Edison records "Mary had a little lamb" and plays it back → 1887: Berliner's flat disc can be copied in large numbers → 1935: tape → 1948/49: LP and single → 1963: cassette; 1979: Walkman (music becomes portable) → **1982: first CDs (pressed in Germany in August, on sale in Japan on 1 October); music becomes numbers** → 1995: ".mp3" → 1999: Napster → 2001/2003: iPod and iTunes → 2008: Spotify → 2025: US$31.7bn, about 70% from streaming, 837M subscribers; vinyl back above $1bn in the US.
