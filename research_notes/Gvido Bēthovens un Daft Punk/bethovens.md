# Ludwig van Beethoven: life-story fact notes for a ~70 s school animation

Sources used most: Beethoven-Haus Bonn picture biography (internet.beethoven.de), English Wikipedia articles (main biography, Heiligenstadt Testament, Death of Beethoven, Symphony 3, Symphony 5, Piano Sonata 14, Für Elise, Stieler portrait), National Arts Centre Canada (full Testament translation), History.com, Interlochen Public Radio. Britannica returned 403 on fetch; Britannica facts below are taken only from search snippets and are marked as such.

## 1. Childhood in Bonn: birth, father's training, first concert, court musician

### Takeaway
Beethoven was baptised in Bonn on 17 December 1770 (his birthday is assumed to be 16 Dec, but no document proves it). His father Johann drilled him hard and showed him off as a prodigy. His first known public concert was in Cologne in March 1778, advertised as age 6 although he was 7. As a teenager he worked as assistant organist and viola player at the Elector's court in Bonn.

### Cited Findings
- Baptised 17 Dec 1770 at St Remigius, Bonn. He was born in December 1770 in the house at Bonngasse 363, now the Beethoven-Haus museum. — [Beethoven-Haus picture biography](https://internet.beethoven.de/biography/picturebiography.html)
- His birth date "is generally taken as 16 December, but no document confirms it." — [Wikipedia: Ludwig van Beethoven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig_van_Beethoven)
- His grandfather, also named Ludwig, was a singer at the Bonn court from 1733 and Kapellmeister from 1761. The family was a family of court musicians. — [Beethoven-Haus](https://internet.beethoven.de/biography/picturebiography.html)
- His father Johann was his first teacher: "The regime was harsh and intensive, often reducing him to tears." — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig_van_Beethoven)
- The first public concert was announced for 26 March 1778 in Cologne, and the announcement gave his age as six although he was seven. — [Beethoven-Haus](https://internet.beethoven.de/biography/picturebiography.html). He played "various clavier concertos and trios". Sources commonly say his father knocked a year off to make him look like a Mozart-type prodigy. — [Classic FM timeline](https://www.classicfm.com/composers/beethoven/guides/beethovens-life-timeline-part-1/), [popularbeethoven: child prodigy?](https://www.popularbeethoven.com/?p=315)
- From 1780/81 he studied with Christian Gottlob Neefe and became Neefe's assistant organist. He played viola in the court orchestra to help support the family and was later appointed court organist. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig_van_Beethoven)
- First published work: Nine Variations WoO 63 (Mannheim, 1782). His mother died in 1787 and he found a second home with the Breuning family. — [Beethoven-Haus](https://internet.beethoven.de/biography/picturebiography.html)

### Inferences
- Possible visual scenes: a small boy on a stool at a keyboard, the father looming behind him (tears), then a poster reading "Cologne 1778, age 6" with the 6 crossed out to 7. That gag works visually and is factual.
- Avoid saying "born 16 December" as a hard fact. "Baptised 17 December 1770" or "December 1770" is safe.

### Gaps
- Stories that his drunken father dragged him out of bed at night to practise come from later recollections in the Fischer manuscript. I did not verify these in this session, so present them only as "reported".

## 2. Vienna (1792), Haydn, piano virtuoso and improviser

### Takeaway
In November 1792 he left Bonn to study with Haydn in Vienna and never returned. He became famous in aristocratic salons as a fiery piano virtuoso and improviser who beat rivals in piano "duels".

### Cited Findings
- On 29 Oct 1792 Count Waldstein wrote in his album. In Nov 1792 Beethoven left Bonn for Vienna to study with Haydn and stayed permanently. — [Beethoven-Haus](https://internet.beethoven.de/biography/picturebiography.html). Waldstein's famous line "receive the spirit of Mozart from the hands of Haydn" was not returned by the fetch, so it is unverified here.
- He moved permanently to Vienna in November 1792 and learned shortly afterwards that his father had died. He studied counterpoint under Haydn. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig_van_Beethoven)
- He made his reputation as a virtuoso pianist and improviser in Viennese aristocratic salons. He won piano "duels" against Joseph Wölfl (1799) and Daniel Steibelt (1800). — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig_van_Beethoven)
- First public "academy" (benefit concert) of his own in Vienna: 2 April 1800 at the Burgtheater. Piano Trios Op. 1 were published by Artaria in 1795. — [Beethoven-Haus](https://internet.beethoven.de/biography/picturebiography.html)
- He also took lessons from Salieri (1801). — [Beethoven-Haus](https://internet.beethoven.de/biography/picturebiography.html)

### Inferences
- Visual: a stagecoach leaving Bonn, then a Vienna salon with Beethoven hammering the keys while the aristocrats are amazed, or a piano "duel" with a rival pianist losing.

### Gaps
- The Haydn–Beethoven relationship was reportedly uneasy. I did not source this in detail.

## 3. Hearing loss: symptoms, Heiligenstadt Testament, conversation books, ear trumpets, piano-leg and rod myths

### Takeaway
His hearing problems began around 1796–1798, and he first confessed them in writing in 1801. In October 1802 he wrote the Heiligenstadt Testament, an unsent letter to his brothers in which he admits he thought of suicide but says his art held him back. From about 1813–14 he used Maelzel's ear trumpets. From about 1818 people had to write to him in "conversation books" (139 survive). The sawn-off piano legs story is a myth. The rod-in-teeth story is plausible but undocumented.

### Cited Findings
- In 1815 he told Charles Neate that his hearing loss began in 1798. By 1801 he was writing to his friends Wegeler and Amenda about it. The probable cause is often given as otosclerosis, but this is not settled. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig_van_Beethoven)
- Some sources say he may have noticed it as early as 1796, in his mid-twenties. — [Euromanticism: Beethoven's ear trumpets](https://www.euromanticism.org/beethovens-ear-trumpets/)
- The first written mention of his deafness is a letter to Wegeler dated 29 June 1801. — [Beethoven-Haus](https://internet.beethoven.de/biography/picturebiography.html)
- **Heiligenstadt Testament.** It was written in Heiligenstadt, a village outside Vienna, on 6 October 1802, with a postscript on 10 October. It is addressed to his brothers Carl and Johann, but Johann's name is left blank. He never sent it. Schindler and Stephan von Breuning found it in his desk in March 1827 and published it that October. Since 1888 it has been held in the Hamburg State and University Library. — [Wikipedia: Heiligenstadt Testament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heiligenstadt_Testament); [Beethoven-Haus](https://internet.beethoven.de/biography/picturebiography.html)
  - Key lines (English translation):
    - "speak louder, shout, for I am deaf"
    - Someone near him "heard a flute in the distance and I heard nothing … someone heard the shepherd singing and again I heard nothing"
    - "little more and I would have put an end to my life – only art it was that withheld me"
    - It ends with "Farewell and do not wholly forget me when I am dead"
    - [NAC-CNA full translation](https://nac-cna.ca/en/stories/story/beethovens-complete-heiligenstadt-testament)
  - Overall: despair over his deafness and isolation, thoughts of suicide, and the decision to live on for his art. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heiligenstadt_Testament)
- **Ear trumpets.** Johann Nepomuk Maelzel (the metronome man) made them in 1812–13. Four are in the Beethoven-Haus Bonn and one is in the Gesellschaft der Musikfreunde in Vienna. Beethoven used them from about 1814. After a quarrel with Maelzel he called them "very poor" but kept using them for years. A Viennese publisher kept a silver ear trumpet in its shop for him. — [Beethoven-Haus](https://internet.beethoven.de/biography/picturebiography.html); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig_van_Beethoven); [Euromanticism](https://www.euromanticism.org/beethovens-ear-trumpets/); [DPMA](https://dpma.de/english/our_office/publications/milestones/inventionsthatmadehistory/beethoven/index.html)
- He kept performing in public as a pianist at least occasionally until about 1814. Deafness ended his career as a touring virtuoso and pushed him toward composing large works. — [Euromanticism](https://www.euromanticism.org/beethovens-ear-trumpets/)
- **Conversation books.** He started using them about 1818. 139 survive: 137 in the Berlin State Library and 2 in the Beethoven-Haus. Visitors wrote their side of the conversation and Beethoven answered out loud. Schindler is believed to have forged entries in some of them. — [Beethoven-Haus](https://internet.beethoven.de/biography/picturebiography.html); [Euromanticism / search summary](https://www.euromanticism.org/beethovens-ear-trumpets/); [Wikipedia: Symphony 5 (Schindler forgeries)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symphony_No._5_(Beethoven))
- He was not completely deaf until the very end. In 1825 he could still understand people who spoke loudly and clearly. Theodore Albrecht's research suggests some hearing remained in his left ear, but this is contested. — [Euromanticism](https://www.euromanticism.org/beethovens-ear-trumpets/); [Audiology Worldnews](https://www.audiology-worldnews.com/research/3726-beethoven-finally-gets-his-hearing-back-for-the-9th)
- **Piano legs: myth.** Interlochen Public Radio's myths-and-facts piece asks "Did Beethoven really saw the legs off his piano so he could feel the vibrations on the floor?" and answers "No." — [Interlochen Public Radio](https://www.interlochenpublicradio.org/classical-music/2021-03-04/beethoven-myths-and-facts-with-iprs-amanda-sewell). Popular sites such as Discover Magazine repeat it without a primary source. — [Discover](https://discovermagazine.com/mind/my-other-ear-is-a-tooth-bone-conduction-helps-the-hard-of-hearing)
- **Rod in the teeth: plausible but uncertain.** Discover says he clenched a stick in his teeth with the other end touching the piano, then admits "we aren't sure" how much he heard this way. A teaching resource calls it "theorized". — [Discover](https://discovermagazine.com/mind/my-other-ear-is-a-tooth-bone-conduction-helps-the-hard-of-hearing); [NF Schools lesson](https://www.nfschools.net/cms/lib/NY19000301/Centricity/Domain/2488/Distance%20Learning%20Lesson%202-Ludwig%20Van%20Beethoven.docx)
- In late 1817 the London firm Broadwood gave him a grand piano. — [Beethoven-Haus](https://internet.beethoven.de/biography/picturebiography.html)

### Inferences
- Strong, accurate visual beats:
  1. The flute or shepherd scene, which comes straight from the Testament: a friend points at a distant shepherd with musical notes floating by, and Beethoven sees nothing.
  2. Fading sound: the notes turn grey and dissolve.
  3. A writing-desk scene with a candle and the letter, using the line "only art held me back."
  4. Ear trumpet up to his ear.
  5. People writing in a notebook to him.
- Using the rod in his teeth is acceptable if it is framed as "he is said to have…". Do not show sawing off piano legs.
- For schoolchildren, describe the suicide content gently, for example "he was in despair, but decided to live for his music."

### Gaps
- No primary source for the rod in the teeth was located. The usual source chain goes back to 19th-century anecdote.

## 4. Key works and dates

### Takeaway
Moonlight Sonata (1801), the Eroica with its Napoleon title scratched out (1803–04), the Fifth Symphony (premiered 22 Dec 1808 in a freezing 4-hour concert), Für Elise (27 April 1810, published only in 1867) and the Ninth (7 May 1824, where the deaf composer was turned round to see the applause).

### Cited Findings
- **Moonlight Sonata** (Piano Sonata No. 14, Op. 27/2). Composed in 1801 and published in 1802 by Cappi in Vienna. It is dedicated to his pupil Countess Giulietta Guicciardi. The original title is "Sonata quasi una fantasia". Beethoven did not call it "Moonlight": the critic Ludwig Rellstab made the moonlight comparison in 1824, after which the nickname spread. — [Wikipedia: Piano Sonata No. 14](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piano_Sonata_No._14_(Beethoven))
- **Symphony No. 3 "Eroica".**
  - It was composed mainly in 1803–04, with sketches from 1802. It was first titled "Bonaparte". — [Wikipedia: Symphony No. 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symphony_No._3_(Beethoven))
  - Ferdinand Ries says that when Beethoven heard Napoleon had made himself emperor (1804), he exclaimed "So he is no more than a common mortal!", tore the title page in half and threw it on the floor. — [Wikipedia: Symphony No. 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symphony_No._3_(Beethoven))
  - A surviving copy in the Gesellschaft der Musikfreunde shows "Intitolata Bonaparte" scratched out (so hard it made a hole, per common accounts, not confirmed in the fetched text). — [Wikipedia: Symphony No. 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symphony_No._3_(Beethoven))
  - It was published in 1806 "to celebrate the memory of a great man". — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig_van_Beethoven)
  - Private premiere on 9 June 1804 at Prince Lobkowitz's palace. Public premiere on 7 April 1805 at the Theater an der Wien. — [Wikipedia: Symphony No. 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symphony_No._3_(Beethoven))
- **Symphony No. 5.**
  - Sketched from 1804 and finished in 1807–08. — [Wikipedia: Symphony No. 5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symphony_No._5_(Beethoven))
  - Premiered on 22 Dec 1808 at the Theater an der Wien in an all-Beethoven concert that also premiered the 6th Symphony and lasted more than 4 hours. The hall was "extremely cold". There was only one rehearsal and the playing was poor. Beethoven stopped and restarted the Choral Fantasy and was heard shouting "badly played, wrong, again!" — [Wikipedia: Symphony No. 5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symphony_No._5_(Beethoven)); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig_van_Beethoven)
  - The "Thus Fate knocks at the door!" quote comes from Schindler and is unreliable. — [Wikipedia: Symphony No. 5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symphony_No._5_(Beethoven))
- **Für Elise** (WoO 59).
  - Nohl saw the now-lost autograph headed "Für Elise am 27 April [1810]". It was published only in 1867, 40 years after his death. — [Wikipedia: Für Elise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F%C3%BCr_Elise)
  - "Elise" is unknown. It may be a misreading of "Therese" (Malfatti), or possibly Elisabeth Röckel. — [Wikipedia: Für Elise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F%C3%BCr_Elise)
- **Symphony No. 9.**
  - Premiered on 7 May 1824 at the Theater am Kärntnertor in Vienna. — [History.com](https://www.history.com/this-day-in-history/may-7/beethoven-ninth-symphony-debuts-vienna)
  - It was performed together with parts of the Missa solemnis and is the first major choral symphony. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig_van_Beethoven)
  - Beethoven stood on stage giving the tempos, while Michael Umlauf actually led the orchestra (commonly reported; Umlauf not confirmed in the fetched text).
  - The contralto Caroline Unger (aged 20) turned him round to face the audience. People raised their hats and handkerchiefs in the air so that he could see the applause, and there were five ovations. — [History.com](https://www.history.com/this-day-in-history/may-7/beethoven-ninth-symphony-debuts-vienna); [The Vintage News](https://www.thevintagenews.com/2016/09/10/premiere-symphony-no-9-beethoven-physically-turned-around-see-audience-applaud-even-waved-handkerchiefs)
  - Accounts disagree on when this happened: after the Scherzo or at the very end. Schindler credits the soprano Sontag in one place and Unger elsewhere. — [search summary of History.com / allclassical](https://research.allclassical.org/?p=1823)
- Other milestones:
  - Opera Fidelio (as Leonore) premiered in Nov 1805.
  - From 1 March 1809 he received an annuity of 4,000 florins from Archduke Rudolph and Princes Kinsky and Lobkowitz, which let him stay in Vienna as a freelance composer.
  - The Missa solemnis was completed in Jan 1823.
  - [Beethoven-Haus](https://internet.beethoven.de/biography/picturebiography.html)

### Inferences
- The Ninth premiere is the emotional climax: silence (the audience muted from his point of view), a singer gently turns him, and he sees a sea of waving hats and handkerchiefs. It is factual enough to show, and it was arranged visually precisely because he could not hear.
- The Eroica tear is a great 2-second beat: a quill scratches out "BONAPARTE" and the page is ripped in two.

### Gaps
- Exact moment of Unger's turn is disputed. Show it at the end to keep it simple, and note that some accounts say the scherzo.

## 5. Death (26 March 1827) and funeral

### Takeaway
He died in Vienna on the afternoon of 26 March 1827, aged 56, after months in bed with liver disease. A friend reported a thunderclap at the moment of death, but that account was given decades later. Roughly 10,000–30,000 people lined the funeral route on 29 March. The Beethoven-Haus and Britannica say about 20,000.

### Cited Findings
- He died on 26 March 1827 in Vienna, aged 56, after several months bedridden. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig_van_Beethoven)
- **Thunderstorm.** Anselm Hüttenbrenner, who was in the room with Beethoven's sister-in-law, described around 5 pm a "startling, awful peal of thunder". Beethoven raised his head and stretched out his right arm "majestically", like a general. — [Wikipedia: Death of Beethoven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_of_Ludwig_van_Beethoven)
  - The "shook his fist at the heavens" version is a later embellishment.
  - The account was given in 1860, and scholars treat its emotional details with caution.
- He wrote his will on 23 March. On 24 March he reportedly said "Plaudite, amici, comoedia finita est" ("Applaud, friends, the comedy is over"). When wine arrived from the publisher Schott he whispered "Pity – too late." — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig_van_Beethoven); [Beethoven-Haus](https://internet.beethoven.de/biography/picturebiography.html)
- **Cause of death.** The autopsy found a severely cirrhotic liver. A 2023 hair-genome study found genetic liver-risk factors plus hepatitis B, possibly interacting with alcohol. The lead-poisoning theory is called dubious. — [Wikipedia: Death of Beethoven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_of_Ludwig_van_Beethoven)
- **Funeral.**
  - Held on 29 March 1827, from the Alserkirche to the Währing cemetery. In 1888 his remains were moved to the Vienna Central Cemetery. — [search summary: Death of Beethoven / classical-music.com](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_of_Ludwig_van_Beethoven)
  - Crowd: "about 20,000 mourners follow the coffin. Eight Kapellmeisters hold the ribbons, Schubert among the torchbearers." — [Beethoven-Haus](https://internet.beethoven.de/biography/picturebiography.html)
  - Other figures: Britannica also gives about 20,000 (search snippet only). [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig_van_Beethoven) says "estimated 10,000". The [Death of Beethoven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_of_Ludwig_van_Beethoven) article gives 10,000–30,000 onlookers. [Classical-music.com](https://www.classical-music.com/features/composers/beethovens-funeral-how-the-composers-final-farewell-attracted-more-attendees-than-michael-jacksons) gives 10,000–30,000.
  - Theatres were closed. Franz Grillparzer wrote the eulogy. — [Wikipedia: Death of Beethoven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_of_Ludwig_van_Beethoven)

### Inferences
- Safe narration: "When he died in 1827, around 20,000 people came to his funeral." Alternatively: "tens of thousands of people".
- Present the thunder as "it is said a thunderstorm raged". A raised arm toward the sky is acceptable as legend.

### Gaps
- None material.

## 6. Appearance (for a recognizable stick-figure caricature)

### Takeaway
Beethoven was short and stocky, about 1.60–1.68 m, with a large head, a ruddy, pock-marked face and a famously wild mane of hair: jet-black when young, grey by 1820. The iconic Stieler portrait (1820) shows him in a dark blue coat with a big white collar and a red scarf, frowning and writing the Missa solemnis.

### Cited Findings
- **Height** is uncertain. Estimates run from 160–165 cm (5'2"–5'5"). Schindler estimated about 1.68 m, and the 1863 exhumation reportedly supports this. Some say he was average height. — [popularbeethoven: How tall](https://www.popularbeethoven.com/?p=1236); [beethoven.ru: How tall](https://www.beethoven.ru/en/node/1313)
- **Build and face.** He was described as short and stocky with a large head, a pockmarked face and a ruddy complexion. One woman remembered him as "short and insignificant with an ugly red face full of pockmarks". He was "broadly made, vigorous, muscular", with a strong torso and short legs. — [popularbeethoven: appearance](https://www.popularbeethoven.com/?p=3167); [WRTI](https://www.wrti.org/post/these-are-quirks-about-beethoven-you-may-not-know)
- **Hair.** One contemporary described it as "untouched by scissors or comb". — [popularbeethoven: appearance](https://www.popularbeethoven.com/?p=3167)
- **Czerny in 1801** described a shaggy grey jacket, bristling jet-black hair and an unshaven beard. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig_van_Beethoven)
- **Stieler portrait** (1820, original in the Beethoven-Haus Bonn). — [Wikipedia: Beethoven (Stieler)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beethoven_(Stieler)); [Beethoven-Haus](https://internet.beethoven.de/biography/picturebiography.html)
  - Clothing: deep blue frock coat, large white collar, red scarf.
  - Hair: grey, "unruly, essentially uncombable", "writhing wildly".
  - Pose: head lowered, holding the Missa solemnis manuscript and seemingly writing. The background is a grape arbour.
  - Sittings: only 3–4.

### Inferences
- **Caricature recipe:**
  1. A big round head on a small, stocky body with short legs.
  2. A huge spiky or cloud-like hair blob sticking out in all directions: dark for scenes of the young man, grey or white for the late scenes.
  3. Heavy, angled eyebrows in a scowl, a downturned mouth and a strong square jaw.
  4. A dark (blue or black) long coat, a white high collar or neckcloth, and an optional red scarf as a colour accent.
  5. Props: a quill or music sheet, an ear trumpet in later scenes, a piano.
- Ageing cue: dark hair in Bonn and early Vienna, greying after about 1815, wild grey hair by 1824.

### Gaps
- Exact height is not verifiable. Use "shortish" rather than a number. Hair colour in old age is not fully covered by my sources beyond "grey" in the 1820 Stieler portrait.

## 7. Why he matters

### Takeaway
Beethoven is the bridge from the Classical style of Haydn and Mozart to Romanticism. He also became the model of the independent artist-genius who composed for himself and the public rather than as a court servant.

### Cited Findings
- He was a key driver of the transition from Classical to Romantic music. As early as 1810 E. T. A. Hoffmann named him, alongside Haydn and Mozart, as one of the three "Romantic" composers. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig_van_Beethoven)
- **Independence.**
  - Unlike his grandfather and father, who were salaried court musicians, he lived in Vienna as a freelance composer. In 1809 three noble patrons promised him a 4,000-florin annuity to keep him from taking a court job in Kassel. — [Beethoven-Haus](https://internet.beethoven.de/biography/picturebiography.html)
  - He replaced virtuoso touring with large works and publisher contracts. — [Euromanticism](https://www.euromanticism.org/beethovens-ear-trumpets/)
- The Ninth was the first major choral symphony. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig_van_Beethoven)

### Inferences
- **Point for the Latvian school audience:** The "Ode to Joy" from the Ninth is the anthem of the European Union, which Latvia belongs to. This is general knowledge and was not separately sourced in this session.
- **Core message:** he composed his greatest music while going deaf.

### Gaps
- No source fetched for the EU anthem (Council of Europe 1972 / EU 1985). Verify before using it in the script.

## 8. Myths to avoid

### Takeaway
Several popular "facts" are false or shaky. For a school film, do not show them as fact, or flag them as legends.

### Cited Findings
- **Sawing off piano legs:** false. — [Interlochen Public Radio](https://www.interlochenpublicradio.org/classical-music/2021-03-04/beethoven-myths-and-facts-with-iprs-amanda-sewell)
- **"Fate knocking at the door" (Fifth Symphony):** reported by Schindler, who is unreliable and is believed to have forged conversation-book entries. — [Wikipedia: Symphony No. 5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symphony_No._5_(Beethoven))
- **The "Moonlight" title:** not Beethoven's own. It comes from Rellstab after 1824. — [Wikipedia: Sonata 14](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piano_Sonata_No._14_(Beethoven))
- **"Born 16 December":** assumed, not documented. Only the baptism (17 Dec) is documented. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig_van_Beethoven)
- **Shaking his fist at the thunderstorm:** a later embellishment of Hüttenbrenner's 1860 account (raised right arm). — [Wikipedia: Death of Beethoven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_of_Ludwig_van_Beethoven)
- **"Totally deaf from his 20s / heard nothing at all":** wrong. The loss was gradual from about 1798. He performed until about 1814 and could still understand loud, clear speech in 1825. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig_van_Beethoven); [Euromanticism](https://www.euromanticism.org/beethovens-ear-trumpets/)
- **Died of lead poisoning:** dubious. Liver disease (cirrhosis, hepatitis B, alcohol) is the leading explanation. — [Wikipedia: Death of Beethoven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_of_Ludwig_van_Beethoven)
- **"Für Elise" written for a known Elise:** the identity is unknown and the title may be a misreading of "Therese". — [Wikipedia: Für Elise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F%C3%BCr_Elise)
- **Unger turning him after the finale:** the timing is disputed (scherzo vs end), and Schindler muddles who did it. — [History.com](https://www.history.com/this-day-in-history/may-7/beethoven-ninth-symphony-debuts-vienna)

### Inferences
- **Suggested ~70 s beat list (all factual):**
  1. 1770 Bonn: a baby and a church baptism.
  2. A strict father at the keyboard, then the Cologne 1778 poster with "6" corrected to "7".
  3. 1792: coach to Vienna, meeting Haydn.
  4. A salon virtuoso, the crowd wowed.
  5. About 1798–1802: the sound fades, the shepherd scene, the Heiligenstadt letter.
  6. 1803–04: the Eroica title torn ("Bonaparte" ripped).
  7. 1808: a freezing hall and the Fifth Symphony's da-da-da-DUM.
