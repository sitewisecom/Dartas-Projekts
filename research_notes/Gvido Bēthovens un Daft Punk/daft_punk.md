# Daft Punk (students wrote "Draft Punk": the correct name is DAFT Punk)

## Members, formation, name and split

### Takeaway
Daft Punk was a French electronic duo of Thomas Bangalter and Guy-Manuel de Homem-Christo. They formed in Paris in 1993 out of the short-lived band Darlin'. Their name came from a mocking Melody Maker review. They split on 22 Feb 2021 with the video "Epilogue", after 28 years together.

### Cited Findings
- French electronic duo formed in Paris in 1993 by Thomas Bangalter and Guy-Manuel de Homem-Christo, who met in 1987 at the Lycée Carnot secondary school. — [Wikipedia: Daft Punk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daft_Punk)
- Their earlier band Darlin' (1992, with Laurent Brancowitz, later of Phoenix) was named after the Beach Boys song "Darlin'" and broke up after about six months. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daft_Punk)
- Name origin: Melody Maker critic Dave Jennings called Darlin's music "a daft, punky thrash". The pair found it funny and took "Daft Punk" as their new name. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daft_Punk)
- Split: on 22 February 2021 they posted an 8-minute YouTube video called "Epilogue". It reuses a scene from their film *Electroma*: one robot explodes, the other walks away, and a title card reads "1993–2021". They gave no reason. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daft_Punk); [PBS NewsHour, 22 Feb 2021](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/arts/grammy-winning-duo-daft-punk-break-up-after-28-years); [Dazed](https://www.dazeddigital.com/music/article/52004/1/daft-punk-announce-split-with-8-minute-video-of-them-exploding-epilogue)
- A group publicist confirmed the split. Secondary reports name her as Kathryn Frazier, who confirmed it to the BBC. — [PBS](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/arts/grammy-winning-duo-daft-punk-break-up-after-28-years); [Taiwan News](https://www.taiwannews.com.tw/en/news/4134116)

### Inferences
- For a Latvian caption, write "Daft Punk" exactly. "Daft" is British slang for "silly". It is not "Draft".

### Gaps
- I did not fetch the original BBC article directly.

## Albums and years (beat 1: digital-era group)

### Takeaway
They released four studio albums: *Homework* (1997), *Discovery* (2001), *Human After All* (2005) and *Random Access Memories* (2013). They also made the live album *Alive 2007* and the *TRON: Legacy* film soundtrack (2010). Their career runs from the late-1990s rise of home computer music to the streaming era.

### Cited Findings
- Studio albums: *Homework* (1997), *Discovery* (2001), *Human After All* (2005), *Random Access Memories* (2013). — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daft_Punk)
- Other releases: *Tron: Legacy* (2010 soundtrack), *Electroma* (2006 film), *Interstella 5555* (2003 film), *Daft Club* (2003 remix album), *Alive 2007* (live album). — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daft_Punk)
- *Homework* and *Discovery* were really recorded in Bangalter's bedroom. Bangalter confirmed this ("It's true") on BBC Sounds' *The First Time…* podcast. Both albums were mixed and recorded through a small JVC boombox with a basic graphic equaliser, a gift from his parents for his 11th birthday, which he called "the magic one". — [MusicTech](https://musictech.com/news/music/daft-punk-homework-discovery-bedroom/)
- Nuance for *Random Access Memories* (2013): they deliberately used live session musicians. These included Nile Rodgers and Paul Jackson Jr. (guitar), Nathan East (bass), and Omar Hakim and John "J.R." Robinson (drums). The album has only one sample and drum machines on just two tracks. Bangalter told Rolling Stone: "We wanted to do what we used to do with machines and samplers but with people." — [Sound & Vision](https://www.soundandvision.com/content/week-music-may-21-2013-techno-trousers-shed-daft-punk); [Spin](https://www.spinmagazine.com/2013/04/daft-punk-random-access-memories-interview-get-lucky-promo-video/)
- RAM guests also included Giorgio Moroder, Pharrell Williams, Julian Casablancas, Panda Bear, Todd Edwards, Paul Williams, Chilly Gonzales and DJ Falcon. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daft_Punk)

### Inferences
- The idea of a "digital-era group" fits *Homework*, *Discovery* and *Human After All* best. RAM was partly a return to analogue and human playing, though it still used synths and vocoders. The script should not claim RAM was made only by computers.

### Gaps
- None critical.

## Sound and techniques (beat 2: house + disco + funk with machines)

### Takeaway
They made French house (also called "French touch" or filter house). They looped disco and funk samples, filtered them, and set them over drum machines and synths. Voices were robotised with vocoders and Auto-Tune.

### Cited Findings
- *Homework* gear, from a 1997 Japanese *Sound & Recording* interview: Roland Juno-106, MC-202, MKS-80, TB-303 and Sequential Prophet-VS synths; TR-707, TR-808 and TR-909 drum machines and a LinnDrum; a Roland S-760 sampler; and a Macintosh computer. The final mix went from the S-760 to DAT. — [Mixmag](https://mixmag.net/read/daft-punk-homework-tech-gear-equipment-news); [MusicTech](https://musictech.com/news/daft-punk-gear-list-1997-homework-album/)
- The "Da Funk" bassline was played on a Roland TB-303. — [Mixmag/MusicTech gear list](https://musictech.com/news/daft-punk-gear-list-1997-homework-album/); [Reverb Machine](https://reverbmachine.com/blog/daft-punk-homework-synth-sounds/)
- Vocal effects named in the main article: Auto-Tune, a Roland SVC-350 vocoder and a DigiTech Vocalist. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daft_Punk)
- "One More Time" vocals are auto-tuned and processed. This is cited as typical of French house's use of audio filters. Bangalter called Auto-Tune "just a tool" and compared the backlash to 1960s criticism of the electric guitar. — [Wikipedia: One More Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_More_Time_(Daft_Punk_song))
- "Harder, Better, Faster, Stronger" vocals are vocoded. — [Wikipedia: HBFS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harder,_Better,_Faster,_Stronger)
- Popular claim to treat carefully: the "Da Funk" lead is often said to be a Korg MS-20, but the MS-20 is not on Daft Punk's own *Homework* or *Discovery* gear lists. — [Reverb Machine](https://reverbmachine.com/blog/daft-punk-homework-synth-sounds/)

### Inferences
- A good visual list for the animation: drum machine, synth keyboard, sampler, a computer (Mac), and a vocoder or microphone with robotic sound waves.

### Gaps
- I could not confirm a talk box (for example a DigiTech Talker) from a quality source in this research. Prefer "vocoder and Auto-Tune".
- I found no source on sidechain or "pumping" compression. Do not state it as fact.

## The three famous tracks (beat 3)

### Takeaway
"One More Time" (Nov 2000) was a No. 1 hit in France. "Harder, Better, Faster, Stronger" (2001) won a Grammy in its live version and became world-famous through Kanye West's "Stronger". "Get Lucky" (2013) was their biggest pop hit and won Record of the Year.

### Cited Findings
- **One More Time**
  - Released 13 November 2000 (Virgin) as the lead single from *Discovery*; recorded in 1998. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_More_Time_(Daft_Punk_song))
  - Samples the horn section of Eddie Johns' "More Spell on You" (1978). — same
  - Vocals were written and sung by the American DJ Romanthony (Anthony Moore). — same
  - Peaks: No. 1 in France, Canada, Portugal and on the Eurochart Hot 100; No. 2 in the UK; No. 1 on US Dance Club Songs; No. 61 on the Billboard Hot 100. — same
- **Harder, Better, Faster, Stronger**
  - Single released 13 October 2001, the 4th single from *Discovery*. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harder,_Better,_Faster,_Stronger)
  - Built on a keyboard riff from Edwin Birdsong's "Cola Bottle Baby" (1979). — same
  - Vocals are vocoded. — same
  - Sampled prominently in Kanye West's "Stronger" (*Graduation*, 2007). Daft Punk performed with West at the 50th Grammys in 2008. — same
  - The *Alive 2007* version won Best Dance Recording at the 51st Grammys. — same
  - The original peaked at No. 25 in the UK. — same
- **Get Lucky** (featuring Pharrell Williams and Nile Rodgers)
  - Released 19 April 2013 as the lead single from *Random Access Memories*. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Get_Lucky_(Daft_Punk_song))
  - Pharrell sings lead; Nile Rodgers plays the defining guitar riff. Daft Punk contributed synths and vocoder vocals. — same
  - No. 1 in the UK for four weeks. It was the UK's second best-selling single of 2013 (1,308,007 copies). — same
  - No. 2 on the Billboard Hot 100 for five weeks and their first US top-10 hit. — same
  - Set the Spotify record for most plays of a song in a single day. Certified diamond in France and 8x Platinum (RIAA, 2023). — same
  - Won Record of the Year and Best Pop Duo/Group Performance at the 56th Grammys in 2014. — same

### Inferences
- "One More Time" fits a disco and party visual. HBFS fits a robotic, technology visual because its lyrics are "work it, make it, do it, makes us...". "Get Lucky" fits a funk-guitar and summer visual.

### Gaps
- Eddie Johns was never credited or paid for the sample (Wikipedia). This is a sensitive detail, probably best left out of a children's animation.

## Robot helmets (beat 4): visual reference

### Takeaway
They first appeared as robots in 1999, and the robots launched the *Discovery* era. **Thomas Bangalter = SILVER/chrome helmet and silver gloves; red LED text scrolls across his dark visor.** **Guy-Manuel = GOLD helmet and gold gloves; dark visor with colourful LED pictures (heart, rain) instead of words.** They chose robots as long-term characters, not just disguises.

### Cited Findings
- They first appeared as robots in 1999. Before that they hid behind Halloween masks or bags. Bangalter wore a silver helmet and gloves; Homem-Christo wore a gold helmet and gloves. The helmets could show various LED effects. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daft_Punk)
- The design came from directors Alex & Martin (Alex Courtès and Martin Fougerol) with Paul Hahn (Daft Arts). Engineering was by Tony Gardner's effects studio Alterian Inc.; Spike Jonze recommended Gardner. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daft_Punk); [Alterian, "Discovery at 20"](https://alterianinc.com/?p=9752)
- Thomas's helmet shows actual text through an LED display, controlled from a keypad on his arm. Gardner: "he's also a very communicative person, so that's why he has literal text on his face." An early concept gave Thomas a red visor. — [Alterian](https://alterianinc.com/?p=9752); [Creative Boom](https://www.creativeboom.com/inspiration/daft-punk/)
- Guy-Manuel's helmet never shows words, only pictographs such as a heart or falling rain. His early concept had a gold, NASA-style visor, but it reflected the whole room and was impossible to photograph, so the finish was dulled. The faceplate has an inner tight black mesh so he can see out but nobody can see in, and the LEDs shine through it. — [Creative Boom](https://www.creativeboom.com/inspiration/daft-punk/); [Alterian](https://alterianinc.com/?p=9752)
- Construction: vacuum-metallised fibreglass shells; the visor tint came from an Ohio company that makes astronaut helmets; backpacks under the clothes powered the LEDs; ribbed hoods were made of foam latex. — [Alterian](https://alterianinc.com/?p=9752)
- Why robots: the duo wanted personas that were "more specific and long-term" than masks used only to hide their identity. Gardner: "They had an idea they wanted to be robots." Early influences were *The Man Who Fell to Earth* and *Phantom of the Paradise*. The robots were first drawn as anime-style characters for *Interstella 5555*, then "brought to life" for *Discovery*. — [Alterian](https://alterianinc.com/?p=9752); [Creative Boom](https://www.creativeboom.com/inspiration/daft-punk/)
- The "sampler explosion" story is a playful legend the group told the press: they said a sampler exploded at 9:09 a.m. on 9 September 1999 and turned them into robots. Bangalter later attributed it to the "9999 bug". — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daft_Punk)

### Inferences
- Drawing guide: two slim figures in black outfits. Left (Thomas): smooth chrome-silver helmet with a dark visor across the face, red LED letters scrolling on it, silver gloves. Right (Guy-Man): rounder gold helmet with a dark, slightly reflective visor, small colourful LED icons (heart, rain), gold gloves. No faces are ever visible.
- Message for beat 4: a human musician hidden inside a machine shell. This matches the album title *Human After All*.

### Gaps
- Black leather suits (Discovery/Alive era) and Hedi Slimane-designed sequinned suits (RAM era) are widely reported, but I did not verify them from a fetched source. Keep the costume simply "black outfits".
- The helmets changed between eras (1999–2001, 2005–07, 2013). The exact LED colours and visor shapes of each era were not documented in what I fetched.
- Wording of the explosion-story date: Wikipedia gives "9:09 a.m. on 9 September 1999". The brief's "9:99 am" is not an accurate quote.

## Influence and spreading a new music era (beat 5)

### Takeaway
Their 2006 Coachella show, played from an LED pyramid, is widely called the "tipping point" for the US EDM boom. It started an "arms race" in festival light shows. They won 6 Grammys and also crossed into anime (*Interstella 5555* with Leiji Matsumoto) and film (*TRON: Legacy*).

### Cited Findings
- The Alive 2006/2007 tour (48 dates) played a "megamix" from a large LED-fronted pyramid. The 2006 Coachella set appears in the documentary *Coachella: 20 Years in the Desert* (2020). — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daft_Punk)
- DJ Mag, citing Spin's Philip Sherburne, called the 2006 Coachella set "the tipping point" for EDM in the US. Its sound-and-light spectacle was particularly influential and showed that electronic musicians could play sets of their own music. — [DJ Mag](https://djmag.com/longreads/how-daft-punks-discovery-laid-foundations-edm)
- The pyramid "lit the fuse for the LED arms race" behind the EDM boom. — [Thump/Vice](https://thump.vice.com/en_au/article/daft-punks-legendary-pyramid-is-only-half-the-story-of-that-fateful-night-at-coachella-2006); [Water & Music](https://www.waterandmusic.com/15-years-ago-daft-punks-coachella-set-helped-spark-an-arms-race-for-live-music-technology)
- *Interstella 5555* is a feature-length anime set to *Discovery*, made with the Japanese manga artist Leiji Matsumoto. Episodes aired on Toonami in 2001, and the DVD came out in 2003. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daft_Punk)
- Grammys: 6 wins in total is the figure widely reported. — [PBS](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/arts/grammy-winning-duo-daft-punk-break-up-after-28-years)
  - 2009: two wins (Best Dance Recording for HBFS live; Best Electronic/Dance Album for *Alive 2007*).
  - 2014: four wins for RAM (Album of the Year, Record of the Year for "Get Lucky", Best Pop Duo/Group Performance, Best Dance/Electronica Album). — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daft_Punk); [Get Lucky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Get_Lucky_(Daft_Punk_song))
- *Discovery* is on Rolling Stone's "500 Greatest Albums of All Time" (2020). — [Wikipedia (citations)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daft_Punk)
- They won the Brit Award for International Group in 2014. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daft_Punk)

### Inferences
- Why the class chose them: they made world hits in a bedroom with a sampler, synths and a computer, and they turned technology itself (robots, LEDs, vocoders) into their image. That makes them a clear symbol of music in the digital era.

### Gaps
- Grammy count conflict: Wikipedia says RAM won 5 Grammys in 2014, while PBS and other news give 6 career wins. The 5th 2014 award, Best Engineered Album, probably went to the engineers rather than the duo, which would explain 2 + 4 = 6. Safe wording: "6 Grammys, including Album of the Year 2014".
- Nomination totals vary between sources (12 or 14). Avoid giving a number.
- The claim that they "started EDM" is contested (Thump, Dance Music NW). Say instead that they "inspired / helped spark".

## Myths to avoid
- "Draft Punk" is wrong; the name is "Daft Punk", from a Melody Maker review. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daft_Punk)
- They were not "real robots". The sampler-explosion story was a joke they told the press. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daft_Punk)
- They did not always wear robot helmets. Before 1999 they used masks or bags. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daft_Punk)
- "Get Lucky"/RAM was not computer-only: it relied on live session musicians. — [Sound & Vision](https://www.soundandvision.com/content/week-music-may-21-2013-techno-trousers-shed-daft-punk)
- The vocals on "One More Time" are Romanthony's, processed. They are not a robot or a Daft Punk member singing. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_More_Time_(Daft_Punk_song))
- "Stronger" is Kanye West's song that samples HBFS. It is not a Daft Punk song. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harder,_Better,_Faster,_Stronger)
- Don't swap the helmet colours: Thomas is silver, Guy-Manuel is gold. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daft_Punk)
